# Purpose

This document defines the governance, permissions, privacy model, audit requirements, and business rules governing the Community Graph.

The Community Graph preserves relationships created through participation while ensuring that individual privacy and community history remain appropriately balanced.

---

# Vision

The Community Graph should become the trusted record of relationships within the worldwide Hash community.

It should preserve history without becoming a surveillance system.

---

# Guiding Principles

- Relationships belong to people.
- History should be preserved.
- Privacy is fundamental.
- Community memory matters.
- Transparency builds trust.

# BR-COMMUNITY-001 — Evidence-Based Relationships

Relationships are created only from verifiable participation.

Examples include:

- Shared Run Sessions
- Officer service
- Trail collaborations
- Event participation
- Mentorship
- Hosted visitor exchanges

Relationships are never inferred solely from messaging, profile views, or proximity.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-002 — Explainable Connections

Every automatically generated relationship recommendation shall include:

- Why it exists
- Supporting events
- Shared Run Capsules
- Shared kennels
- Shared collaborations

Users may dismiss recommendations permanently.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-003 — Privacy Controls

Users control the visibility of:

- Relationship timeline
- Mentorship
- Visitor history
- Hosted guests
- Travel history
- Recognition roles

Private relationships remain excluded from public discovery.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-004 — Historical Preservation

Verified historical participation remains part of the permanent community record where appropriate.

Personal profile visibility changes do not silently alter verified event history.

Where privacy regulations require removal or anonymization, the historical record should preserve event continuity without exposing personal information.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-005 — Recognition Governance

Recognition titles:

- Require published criteria.
- Must reference supporting evidence.
- Are awarded by authorized community governance.
- May be reviewed or revoked through documented processes.

AI may recommend candidates but never grant recognition.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-006 — Legacy Profiles

Legacy Profiles require:

- Verified status.
- Appropriate authorization.
- Respectful presentation.
- Ongoing governance by designated custodians.

Legacy Profiles remain read-only except for approved memorial additions.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-007 — AI Transparency

AI-generated summaries, recommendations, or relationship insights must:

- Be clearly identified.
- Cite supporting records.
- Avoid speculation.
- Respect visibility settings.
- Be reviewable by users.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-008 — Audit Trail

The platform records:

- Relationship creation
- Visibility changes
- Recognition awards
- Mentorship updates
- Legacy status changes
- Administrative actions
- AI-generated summaries

Audit records are immutable.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-009 — Community Stewardship

Kennel officers may manage:

- Official leadership history
- Officer succession
- Heritage recognitions
- Community projects

Officer actions are fully auditable.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-010 — Portability

Users may export:

- Contribution history
- Mentorship history
- Recognition records
- Public relationship summaries
- Community timeline

Exports exclude information belonging to other users unless permission has been granted.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-011 — Accessibility

Community visualizations shall support:

- Keyboard navigation
- Screen readers
- High-contrast themes
- Reduced-motion mode
- Alternative text representations

Accessibility applies to all graph visualizations.

---

# BR-COMMUNITY-012 — Future Expansion

The Community Graph architecture shall support future relationship types, including:

- Educational programs
- Regional councils
- International working groups
- Volunteer initiatives
- Heritage committees

New relationship types should extend the graph without requiring structural redesign.

# Acceptance Criteria

The Community Graph is considered complete when:

- Relationship governance is documented.
- Privacy controls are comprehensive.
- AI recommendations are explainable.
- Legacy Profiles are governed.
- Recognition processes are transparent.
- Audit logging is implemented.

---

# Non-Functional Expectations

The Community Graph should support:

- Tens of millions of members.
- Hundreds of millions of relationships.
- Fast graph traversal.
- Explainable recommendations.
- Privacy-aware indexing.
- Long-term historical preservation.

---

- Cross-community volunteer networks.
- Heritage research workspaces.
- Community impact studies.
