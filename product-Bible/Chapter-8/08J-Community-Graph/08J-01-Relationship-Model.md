# Purpose

This document defines how meaningful relationships are formed, maintained, and visualized within the Hash Community Platform.

Relationships are derived primarily from verified participation rather than explicit friend requests.

The Community Graph reflects real-world interaction while respecting privacy and user control.

---

# Philosophy

Hash friendships are built one trail at a time.

Every shared experience strengthens the graph.

The graph is not a social network.

It is a historical record of community participation.

---

# Guiding Principles

- Relationships emerge naturally.
- Participation creates trust.
- Every relationship has evidence.
- Privacy is always respected.
- Human connections are more important than metrics.

---

# Relationship Formation

Relationships may be established through:

- Participating in the same Run Session.
- Completing the same trail.
- Serving as officers together.
- Laying trails together.
- Writing Trail Reports together.
- Attending Interhash events.
- Visiting the same kennel.
- Hosting visiting Hashers.
- Collaborating on community projects.
- Mentorship relationships.

Every relationship references one or more supporting events.

---

# Relationship Strength

Relationship strength is not shown publicly as a score.

Internally, HCP may calculate relationship confidence using factors such as:

- Number of shared runs.
- Number of shared kennels.
- Shared officer service.
- Shared travel.
- Shared Run Capsules.
- Length of acquaintance.

This internal model improves recommendations but is never exposed as a popularity metric.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-001 — Shared Experiences

## Priority

Critical

Each relationship includes a timeline of shared experiences.

Examples:

- First run together.
- Most recent run.
- Shared anniversary runs.
- Shared Red Dress Runs.
- Shared Interhash events.
- Collaborative trail laying.

Every event links back to the relevant Run Capsule.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-002 — Community Timeline

Users may explore the history of a relationship.

Timeline entries include:

- Runs attended together.
- Officer roles served.
- Trail collaborations.
- Passport milestones.
- Travel history.

The timeline becomes a shared memory rather than a message history.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-003 — Mentorship

Experienced Hashers may optionally become mentors.

Mentorship records include:

- Start date.
- Kennel.
- Shared runs.
- Learning milestones.

Mentorship relationships remain optional and privacy-controlled.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-004 — Visitor Connections

When visiting another kennel, users may optionally record:

- Host Hashers.
- Visitor introductions.
- Local guides.
- Twin kennel exchanges.

These relationships enrich both users' Community Graphs.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-005 — Collaboration

Relationships may include collaborative achievements such as:

- Co-Haring.
- Co-Scribing.
- Event organization.
- Charity initiatives.
- Anniversary planning.

Collaborations are linked to the relevant Run Capsules or events.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-006 — Twin Kennel Relationships

The graph supports official and informal relationships between kennels.

Examples:

- Twin kennels.
- Sister kennels.
- Frequent exchange partners.
- Historic collaborations.

These links become part of the Atlas and community history.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-007 — Community Introductions

With user consent, HCP may suggest introductions based on:

- Mutual shared experiences.
- Planned attendance at the same event.
- Common travel destinations.
- Shared mentors.
- Twin kennel relationships.

Suggestions are informational only.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-008 — Relationship Explorer

Users may visualize their Community Graph through interactive views:

- Personal network.
- Kennel network.
- Travel network.
- Officer network.
- Mentor network.
- Event network.

Visualization focuses on understanding rather than ranking.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-009 — Relationship Privacy

Users control visibility of:

- Shared runs.
- Shared travel.
- Mentorships.
- Visitor history.
- Collaboration history.

Private relationships are excluded from public discovery.

---

# FR-COMMUNITY-010 — Community Memory

Every relationship preserves historical context.

Even if a member becomes inactive, the shared history remains part of the permanent record, subject to privacy and applicable data protection requirements.

---

# Business Principles

Relationships should reflect reality.

Shared experiences matter more than online interactions.

Community grows through participation.

---

# Completion Criteria

Complete when:

- Relationship formation is defined.
- Shared timelines exist.
- Mentorship is supported.
- Visitor relationships are documented.
- Privacy controls are comprehensive.
