# Purpose

This document defines the principles, standards, and technical strategies that enable interoperability between HCP and external systems while ensuring communities retain long-term ownership and portability of their information.

Interoperability strengthens trust by preventing unnecessary vendor dependency and encouraging sustainable collaboration.

---

# Vision

Connected systems.

Open standards.

Communities in control.

---

# Philosophy

Communities should never lose access to their own information because of technology choices.

The platform should communicate effectively with other systems while preserving historical integrity and data quality.

---

# Interoperability Principles

The platform shall:

- Support open standards where practical.
- Encourage structured data exchange.
- Preserve metadata.
- Minimize proprietary dependencies.
- Enable long-term archival formats.
- Maintain semantic consistency.
- Promote responsible integration.

Interoperability enables longevity.

---

# Supported Exchange Formats

Examples include:

- JSON
- CSV
- XML
- PDF/A (archival documents)
- Markdown
- OpenDocument formats
- Standard image metadata
- Standard calendar formats (iCalendar)
- Standard contact formats (vCard)

Additional formats may be adopted over time.

---

# Data Portability

Communities may:

- Export member records.
- Export governance records.
- Export historical archives.
- Export event histories.
- Export media metadata.
- Export audit information (subject to permissions).
- Export AI-generated knowledge where appropriate.

Exports remain understandable.

---

# Integration Strategy

The platform supports:

- REST APIs
- Event webhooks
- Import/export pipelines
- Scheduled synchronization
- Identity federation
- Third-party connectors

Integration remains secure.

---

# Long-Term Preservation

Archival exports should:

- Preserve provenance.
- Preserve relationships.
- Preserve metadata.
- Include documentation.
- Remain usable without proprietary software.

Longevity remains a design objective.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

Interoperability succeeds when:

- Communities exchange information reliably.
- Data exports remain usable.
- Historical integrity is preserved.
- Integrations remain sustainable.
- Platform trust increases.

---

# Completion Criteria

Complete when:

- Interoperability strategy is documented.
- Data portability framework is defined.
- Open standards policy is established.
